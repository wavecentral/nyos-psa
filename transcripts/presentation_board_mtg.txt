I was placed on a couple of different committees. One of the committees that I was placed onto was the board development committee and I was tasked with the concept of putting something together to help not only educate the board but hopefully to inform and inspire the board about the types of facets and the types of concepts that make NIOS I began teaching biology in NIOS in the summer of 2009. Coming fresh off a couple of stints working in large public school districts in both Austin and Lockhart, Texas. Little did I know at the time, but as I would soon find out, NIOS was just exactly the type of school in which I often hoped I could teach. So a few years before, I had been asked this question often as I was progressing through the process of earning official certification to teach science at the secondary level. People would ask, what kind of school do you want to teach? Based on my own educational background, attending some of the largest public schools, as well as some of the smallest colleges and largest universities in my home state, as well as my first experiences with classroom teaching at a tiny private school geared towards students with learning disabilities. Shout out to my first acronym school employer, the CORE Academy, Kids Obtaining Real Education. I was always very ready with an answer to that question. "I would want to teach," I would reply, "at a school with small classes so the teacher and students really get to know one another as people." Folks would nod as though this was the end of my response. "Also," I would continue, "I'd like to work with students who are interested and invested in learning what I have to teach them." Folks would widen their eyes and nod again, agreeing that this was indeed a worthwhile hope to have for a person seeking a teaching career. And I would proceed, never being one to stop short of a full answer when asked, I'd like to have the freedom and support to teach the content the way that I want to teach it, the way that I know how to best deliver the knowledge and skills that the students need. At this assertion, folks who had perhaps only been trying to make idle chit-chat and small talk would add raised eyebrows to their widened eyes and nodding heads in a dual expression of both their recognition that those are wonderful things to wish for and also their reflexive doubt that many such schools exist or that teaching jobs at any of those schools would be easy to come by. 

In the summer of 2009, I found myself in just the right place at just the right time to become part of the school that embodied all three of these fundamental pillars of my personal professional high degree. Not Your Ordinary School was founded upon these very principles and through intentional design and collective of individual efforts continues to strive to provide these elements to every person within the community, teachers and staff, students, parents, and administrators alike. 

For many years since, this absolute gem of a dream situation has provided me opportunities to grow and to learn, improve, innovate, and prosper as a teacher, as a coach, as a leader, but also as a follower, and as a learner, and as a human. For many countless other members of our campus community, whether their tenure has been long or short, no matter what age or role they play on campus, these opportunities have been the same. And it's this collective teaching, learning, and holistic improvement that is the hallmark of the aspects of NIOS that make it not ordinary. I reached out to many other members of the campus community to give feedback for them about their own perspectives and their own experiences, which demonstrate what is not ordinary about our school.
