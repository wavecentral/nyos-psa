The Teachers Speak

What Makes NYOS
Not Ordinary?

In December 2023, NYOS teachers and students made a presentation for the school board about the heart of this place. Three years later, a few parents pulled it back up — to remember, and to ask what we still want to carry forward.

Compiled from a message by Elizabeth Zimmermann & Katie Lee

About this PSA

A few years ago, a group of NYOS middle and high school teachers and students put together a presentation for the school board called What Makes NYOS Not Ordinary? It was made for board members, for new families, and for longtime ones — a record of what this school has been at its best.

Watching it now, three years on, it's a clear-eyed reminder of what we've valued at the core: the family feel and culture, small class sizes, seasoned teachers with autonomy, the modified year-round calendar, chess club, board student-members, traditions like the Goat Parade and Black History Month, and a commitment to teaching the whole child instead of teaching to the test.

The school is growing and changing — that's a fact. But growth doesn't have to mean losing the heart. Below are the two source recordings the PSA refers to, with brief summaries and links to the full transcripts.

The Headliner  ·  NYOS Board Meeting

What Makes NYOS “Not Ordinary”?

A presentation to the school board · December 2023

Video courtesy of Katie Gowell

A NYOS biology teacher and coach — placed on the Board Development Committee and asked to help the board understand what makes this school different — opens the December 2023 presentation with his own story. He started teaching at NYOS in 2009, fresh from large public districts in Austin and Lockhart, and found exactly the school he had been hoping to teach in.

"Small classes so the teacher and students really get to know one another as people. Students who are interested and invested in learning. The freedom and support to teach the way I know how to best deliver it."

He frames these as the three pillars NYOS was founded on, and credits longtime teacher Gloria Ramirez for editing the testimonial video that follows — featuring NYOS teachers, parents, and students from the school's past, present, and future.

From the Teachers & Students

The Testimonial Video

In their own words · NYOS faculty, alumni, and students

A compilation of testimonials from NYOS teachers, alumni, and students. Voices include Lori Ramirez (teaching at NYOS since 2002), Dori Zeitler (secondary theater, nearly ten years), and a NYOS original from the school's very first first-grade class who returned to lead chess club. Together they describe what makes NYOS not ordinary: small class sizes, teacher autonomy, the modified year-round calendar, Marzano-based practice, student board representation, K–12 mentorship, traditions like Black History Month and the Goat Parade, civic engagement and service learning, and a culture that prioritizes the whole child over test scores.

"At any other school, I'm so certain they would have started laughing at her. But our school didn't do that. One person yelled, 'You got this!' And then it was twenty seconds of thunderous applause."

Dori closes with the story of a student who blanked mid–poem at the Black History Month show — and instead of laughs or boos, the audience burst into thunderous applause until she found her words and finished. That, she says, is what makes us special.
