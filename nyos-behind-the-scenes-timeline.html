About this conversation

In May 2026, two NYOS parents sat down on Facebook Live for a public conversation about how decisions get made at our school, what outside firms are doing back-office work, and what's been disclosed — or not — along the way.

The discipline of the conversation was simple: keep what's confirmed in the public record separate from the questions that remain open. No accusations. No speculation about motive. Just IRS filings, board minutes, and other charters' agendas, lined up next to each other so families can see what they show.

What follows is a parent-to-parent summary of the three things Mark and Elizabeth walked through. Every claim is footnoted in the full report linked at the bottom of this page.

The Conversation

Three things the records show.

What it took to start asking these questions, what DSS actually is, and why an undisclosed dual role matters — even if no one has been accused of anything.

01
Why this started

Public records, and a question every parent has a right to ask.

Mark Garcia, on what made him start digging

Mark isn't a journalist or a lawyer. He's a NYOS parent who set out to understand, not investigate. Things at the school had started to feel different — quieter, more top-down — and every question came back as “it's complicated” or no answer at all.

So he started reading what was already public: IRS filings, board agendas, board minutes, vendor lists. Once those documents are lined up next to each other, he says, a story begins to take shape that nobody had walked the community through.

"Who is making decisions about our school, and who is getting paid in the process?"

That's the only question driving this work. Not a hunch, not an agenda — just public records, and a question every NYOS parent has a right to ask.

02
Q1  ·  What is DSS?

A Dallas firm, ongoing payments, and a familiar pattern.

Why parents should care about Dynamic Support Solutions

Dynamic Support Solutions (DSS) is a Dallas firm that sells back-office services — accounting, CFO work, payroll, IT, audit support — to charter schools. Three reasons NYOS parents should care, all from the public record:

1. NYOS is paying DSS. Board minutes show a DSS audit-support contract ratified in December 2024, and a second DSS contract over $50,000 approved in July 2025. DSS is currently listed as a sponsor of the NYOS golf tournament. This is an ongoing financial relationship — not a one-time engagement.

2. The interim leader who steered our recent transition is formally tied to DSS. Dr. Alan Seay — NYOS's Interim Executive Director from February through June 2024 — is listed on DSS's own corporate website as their Special Advisor, School Leadership. He held that title at DSS while running NYOS. He still holds it today.

3. The same playbook had been run before. Public Legacy Traditional Schools — Texas board agendas show Dr. Seay was hired as Legacy's interim superintendent in 2022, and personally co-presented the motion that approved Dr. Mechiel Rozas as Legacy's permanent superintendent. Less than two years later, the same two-person sequence played out at NYOS — Dr. Seay as our interim, Dr. Rozas installed as our permanent leader. Both schools' own board records confirm it.

"DSS isn't some random vendor parents have never heard of. It's a firm with a documented tie to the person who installed our current leadership — and the same playbook had already been run at another Texas charter."
03
Q2  ·  Conflict of interest

Confirmed, undisclosed, and still unanswered.

What the public record shows — and what it can't

In plain terms: a conflict of interest is when somebody making a decision for an organization also has a financial stake on the other side of that decision. By itself, it doesn't mean anyone did anything wrong. It means there's a structural problem the public can't evaluate unless it's disclosed.

Texas law and NYOS's own policy both require board members and key staff to disclose conflicts. NYOS's own IRS filing describes that disclosure as “voluntary.” Here's what the public record shows:

  • NYOS's IRS Form 990 reports paying Dr. Seay zero dollars directly during his interim tenure. Someone else paid him — his consulting LLC, DSS, or both. The public record doesn't say which.
  • During and after his tenure, NYOS has entered multiple contracts with DSS, the firm where he's listed as a Special Advisor.
  • In October 2025 — sixteen months after his interim role ended — Dr. Seay returned to NYOS to lead a paid board training session at ESC Region 13. The board ratified those minutes 12–0. Who paid him for that is also not disclosed.
"This community was never told about the dual role. There was no public disclosure that the person steering our school was simultaneously an advisor to a firm that has since received public dollars from our school."

None of this, by itself, proves wrongdoing. What it shows is an undisclosed dual role — and the only way to answer the remaining questions is the contracts, the disclosure forms, and the payment records, which the Texas Public Information Act gives parents the right to request.
